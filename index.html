I'm excited to announce the NYU FPGA Club.

The future of fast systems is software-hardware co-design.

The future of programming isn’t just writing optimal code to work pretty fast on CPUs. It isn’t merely leveraging caches, clock cycles and multithreading. The future is hardware-software co-design. You design hardware for a specific purpose, and have software for a specific purpose. The leading hardware analysts predict the future to be this hardware-software co-design.

An FPGA is a chip you can rewire with code, turning it into a custom circuit built for exactly one job. FPGAs are already being used by Trading Exchanges, Defense Companies, Data Centers and Robotics Companies. It is also used to process signals from larger systems, such as Telecommunications, Quantum Computing. It enables real time, large scale data processing.

Anywhere there is a system, with speed, scalability or reliability requirements, which happens pretty much with any critical system in the world, FPGA is either applied, or can be applied.

Furthermore, there has never been a greater demand for FPGAs and Custom Chips.

AI runs on custom silicon now. Google, Amazon, Meta and OpenAI all design their own chips. Startups like Etched have propped up, with insane valuations as a testament to the extremely hot demand of this area. Every one of those chips needs engineers who know digital design, and an FPGA is where you learn it.

This huge application and greater demand of FPGA into its own dedicated engineering role calls for the need of a similarly dedicated club. In the NYU FPGA club, we’ll

  • explore the traditional use cases of FPGA development - such as those by HFT firms and defense.
  • invite industry professionals for talks/mentoring.
  • focus on building a repertoire for developing on FPGAs, thus open sourcing the proprietary nature.
  • explore novel use cases for FPGAs in areas such as robotics.

This club is aimed at training its members into developing practical skills, with professional grade projects and industry collaborations.
